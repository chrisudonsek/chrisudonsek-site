Nigeria Has More Internet Users Than Ever. Why Is Youth Unemployment Still Rising?

Between 1993 and 2023, Nigeria went from effectively zero internet users to over 100 million. Mobile subscriptions expanded dramatically. By any measure of digital connectivity, the trajectory has been extraordinary. Over the same period, youth unemployment went in the wrong direction. This is the paradox I spent months studying — and the answer changes how you think about what technology can actually do.

Trust as Infrastructure: Why Africa's Commerce Problem Is Really an Identity Problem

A contractor takes a deposit and disappears. A vendor's listing does not match the product. A "verified" badge means nothing because no one knows who verified it or how. These are not edge cases in African digital commerce. They are the norm.

The conventional response is to call this a trust problem. That framing is imprecise — and the imprecision matters, because it leads to the wrong solutions.

If it is a trust problem, the solution is cultural: build relationships, rely on referrals, wait for reputations to develop. That solution does not scale. If it is an infrastructure problem — and it is — then the solution is to build the layer that makes trust between strangers rational. Build it once, build it well, and every platform that needs it integrates it rather than rebuilding from scratch.

Roughly 80 to 90 percent of employment in Sub-Saharan Africa is informal. These workers have real skills and real experience with no way to prove it to a stranger. Keepdots is the credential the informal economy does not have — not a substitute for skills, but a system that makes skills legible.

Nobody talks about this part. Before you can hire someone, you need employment contracts. Before you can offer equity, you need a corporate structure that can hold it.
